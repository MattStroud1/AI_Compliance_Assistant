# EU AI Act Risk Levels

## Overview

The EU AI Act classifies AI systems into four risk levels based on their potential impact on fundamental rights, safety, and society.

## Risk Levels

### 1. Unacceptable Risk (Prohibited)

**Definition:** AI practices that are considered a clear threat to safety, livelihoods, and rights of people.

**Examples:**
- Subliminal manipulation causing harm
- Social scoring by public authorities
- Real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces (with exceptions for law enforcement)
- Exploiting vulnerabilities of specific groups (age, disability)

**Compliance:** These systems are **banned** under the EU AI Act.

---

### 2. High Risk

**Definition:** AI systems that pose significant risks to health, safety, or fundamental rights.

**Categories:**
1. Biometric identification and categorization
2. Critical infrastructure management
3. Education and vocational training
4. Employment and worker management
5. Access to essential services (credit scoring, benefit eligibility)
6. Law enforcement
7. Migration and border control
8. Administration of justice

**Requirements:**
- Risk management system
- High-quality data governance
- Technical documentation
- Record-keeping (logging)
- Transparency and user information
- Human oversight
- Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity
- Conformity assessment before market placement
- CE marking and EU database registration

---

### 3. Limited Risk (Transparency Obligations)

**Definition:** AI systems that require transparency to allow users to make informed decisions.

**Examples:**
- Chatbots and conversational AI
- Emotion recognition systems
- Biometric categorization systems
- AI-generated content (deepfakes)

**Requirements:**
- Clear disclosure that users are interacting with AI
- Labeling of AI-generated or manipulated content
- Users must be informed about AI use

---

### 4. Minimal Risk

**Definition:** AI systems with minimal or no risk to rights and safety.

**Examples:**
- AI-enabled video games
- Spam filters
- Recommendation systems (in many contexts)

**Requirements:**
- No specific obligations
- Voluntary codes of conduct encouraged
- General product safety requirements apply

---

## Determining Your Risk Level

To classify your AI system:

1. **Check if prohibited:** Does it fall under unacceptable risk categories?
2. **Review high-risk annexes:** Is it listed in Annex III or is it a safety component under existing EU law?
3. **Assess transparency needs:** Does it interact with humans or generate content?
4. **Default to minimal:** If none of the above, it's likely minimal risk

## Timeline

- **August 2024:** EU AI Act enters into force
- **February 2025:** Prohibited practices banned
- **August 2026:** General purpose AI rules apply
- **August 2027:** High-risk system requirements fully applicable
